Mainway

An AI receptionist for clinics in Turkey. It answers in Turkish, books the appointment, and stops talking the moment you cut in.

I built every layer alone. Calls arrive over SIP through Asterisk, twenty milliseconds of audio at a time. Speech-to-text runs while you are still talking, Claude writes the answer one sentence at a time, and ElevenLabs starts on the first sentence before the second one exists. If Claude is slow, three other models are waiting.

It ran on a real Turkish phone number until the server lease ended in October 2026. A browser demo is being built; until then, its own site walks through a call down to the millisecond.

Track 2Out now, open source

brisk

A free Windows cleaner that tells you why your PC is slow, and refuses to claim anything it didn’t measure.

I wanted a cleaner I could trust. Every diagnosis is a rule you can read in the source, and every finding carries the evidence it was built from. It only touches an allowlist, every change can be undone, and there is no telemetry, no account and no AI inside.

Days later it reads its own changes back, to check Windows didn’t quietly undo them. 171 tests keep it honest. One line to install:

Track 3In development, iOS and Android

Booksy TR

Booking for barbers, hairdressers, nail and massage salons in Turkey. You find a free slot today; the shop sees its whole day on one screen.

Two apps on one Supabase backend, one for customers and one for the shop. It began as a clickable prototype: filmed salon scenes behind the search, a headline word that changes with each scene, and a calendar that flags first-time customers so the shop can greet them properly.

Now it is moving into Expo, one screen at a time. The working name is sıradaki, “who’s next”. If you want to click through the prototype, ask me and I’ll send the link.

Track 4In development, private

Red team agent

An agent that hunts for security bugs in bug-bounty programmes, built so it can’t break the rules it’s given.

It wraps the open-source Strix engine in a safety core of my own. Before any request leaves, a scope gate checks the target against the programme’s rules, and the sandbox can only reach hosts on an allowlist.

Findings come to me as drafts, and I decide what gets sent.
